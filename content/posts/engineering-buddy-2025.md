In 2025, I was assigned as an engineering buddy for a new joiner during his 90-day probation period at Kredivo.

The goal of the program was to ensure new engineers could adapt smoothly to the engineering environment — technically, operationally, and culturally — and become productive as early as possible.

New joiners often face challenges such as fragmented documentation, unclear domain boundaries, lack of context around ongoing projects, and difficulty identifying the right PIC for specific topics. Without structured guidance, this can significantly slow down ramp-up time.

## Approach

I supported the onboarding process end-to-end during the 90-day probation period by:

- Helping gather and curate the documentation and requirements needed for his development tasks.
- Providing structured explanations of domain context, especially for the projects he was assigned to.
- Conducting 1-on-1 knowledge sharing sessions to transfer practical engineering insights beyond written documentation.
- Introducing him to relevant team members to make collaboration easier and reduce hesitation in cross-team communication.
- Periodically checking in to identify blockers early and ensure smooth progress.

Rather than waiting for issues to surface, I proactively maintained communication to ensure he felt supported.

## Results

- Accelerated the new joiner's ramp-up time by reducing ambiguity around systems and processes.
- Improved onboarding experience by providing structured domain clarity and direct knowledge transfer.
- Reduced potential delays caused by documentation gaps.
- Strengthened team collaboration by helping him build early working relationships across teams.
- Contributed to a smoother probation journey with consistent guidance and support.

This demonstrates impact beyond individual delivery — contributing to team scalability and long-term engineering capacity.
