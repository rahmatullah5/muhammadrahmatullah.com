## Context and Scope

In 2025, I was assigned as an engineering buddy for a new joiner during his 90-day probation period at Kredivo.

The goal of the program was to ensure new engineers could adapt smoothly to the engineering environment — technically, operationally, and culturally — and become productive as early as possible.

New joiners often face challenges such as:

- Fragmented documentation
- Unclear domain boundaries
- Lack of context around ongoing projects
- Difficulty identifying the right PIC for specific topics

Without structured guidance, this can significantly slow down ramp-up time.

---

## Goals and Non-Goals

### Goals

- Accelerate ramp-up time for the new joiner within the 90-day probation period.
- Provide structured domain context and practical engineering insights.
- Build early working relationships across teams to enable smooth collaboration.
- Identify and resolve blockers proactively.

### Non-Goals

- Not a formal training program (no curriculum or syllabus).
- Not a performance evaluation role — focused purely on support and enablement.
- Not responsible for the new joiner's task assignments or sprint planning.

### Degree of Constraint

- Limited time window (90 days) means prioritization is critical.
- Documentation gaps made it harder to point the new joiner to self-serve resources.
- Cross-team introductions needed to be timed well to avoid overwhelming the mentee.

## Alternatives Considered

| Approach                   | Why Not Chosen                                                  |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Self-serve onboarding only | Documentation gaps made this insufficient                       |
| Rotating buddy system      | Consistency matters — a single point of contact builds trust    |
| Formal training bootcamp   | Too heavyweight for a single new joiner; buddy model is lighter |

---

## Cross-Cutting Concerns

- **Knowledge Transfer**: 1-on-1 sessions covered not just technical topics but also team culture, communication norms, and domain context.
- **Communication**: Regular check-ins to surface blockers early.
- **Documentation**: Helped curate and organize documentation the new joiner needed, improving resources for future hires as well.

---

## Implementation & Impact

I supported the onboarding process end-to-end during the 90-day probation period:

- Helped gather and curate the documentation and requirements needed for his development tasks.
- Provided structured explanations of domain context, especially for the projects he was assigned to.
- Conducted 1-on-1 knowledge sharing sessions to transfer practical engineering insights beyond written documentation.
- Introduced him to relevant team members to make collaboration easier and reduce hesitation in cross-team communication.
- Periodically checked in to identify blockers early and ensure smooth progress.

Rather than waiting for issues to surface, I proactively maintained communication to ensure he felt supported.

**Results:**

- Accelerated the new joiner's ramp-up time by reducing ambiguity around systems and processes.
- Improved onboarding experience by providing structured domain clarity and direct knowledge transfer.
- Reduced potential delays caused by documentation gaps.
- Strengthened team collaboration by helping him build early working relationships across teams.
- Contributed to a smoother probation journey with consistent guidance and support.

This demonstrates impact beyond individual delivery — contributing to team scalability and long-term engineering capacity.
